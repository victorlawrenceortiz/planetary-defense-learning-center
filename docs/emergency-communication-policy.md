# Emergency Communication Policy

The TESS communication policy is simple: verify first, cite sources, speak calmly, and clearly separate education dashboards from official emergency instructions.

## Communication rule

TESS public pages should help people understand science and readiness. They should not sound like an official emergency broadcast unless authorized agencies have issued official instructions.

## Public safety boundary

If a real emergency exists, people should follow local emergency management, national warning systems, NASA/JPL, and other authorized agencies. TESS should point people there.

## Practical workflow

1. **Verify** — confirm information against authoritative sources and timestamps.
2. **Cite** — provide source links when possible so visitors can check the original data.
3. **Use plain language** — explain uncertainty, probability, and context without sensational phrasing.
4. **Separate roles** — label TESS content as education or mission support, not official orders.
5. **Update or retract** — correct stale or wrong information quickly and visibly.
6. **Escalate appropriately** — direct users to official agencies for emergency instructions.

## Approved tone

- Calm, factual, sourced, and practical.
- Clear about uncertainty.
- Focused on what visitors can do next.

## Avoided tone

- Panic language or dramatic countdown claims.
- Unsourced social-media rumors.
- Statements implying TESS is the official emergency authority.
