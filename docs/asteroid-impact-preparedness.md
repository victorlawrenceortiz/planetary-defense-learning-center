# Asteroid Impact Preparedness

Asteroid impact preparedness is best treated as practical all-hazards readiness: communication plans, emergency supplies, trusted alerts, and calm family decision-making.

## Preparedness without panic

A large confirmed impact threat is rare. Still, practical readiness is useful for many hazards: severe weather, outages, evacuations, communications failures, and family emergencies.

## Follow official instructions

If an official alert is issued, follow local emergency management and national warning systems. TESS educational pages can explain context, but they do not replace emergency orders.

## Practical workflow

1. **Build a contact plan** — decide how family members will communicate if phones or internet are unreliable.
2. **Keep basic supplies** — water, food, medicine, lighting, batteries, radio, chargers, documents, and first-aid basics help in many emergencies.
3. **Know local alerts** — enable official local emergency alerts and learn evacuation/shelter guidance for your area.
4. **Reduce misinformation** — share official links and avoid forwarding unsourced claims.
5. **Plan for pets and family needs** — include medication, mobility, accessibility, children, and pet requirements.
6. **Review regularly** — update supplies, contacts, and documents on a repeating schedule.

## Practical supplies

- Water and shelf-stable food.
- Flashlights, batteries, radio, chargers.
- First aid, medications, copies of key documents.

## Practical decisions

- Where to meet.
- Who to contact.
- Which official alert sources to trust.
