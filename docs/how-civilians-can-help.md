# How Civilians Can Help

Civilians can support planetary defense by learning the basics, sharing verified information, preparing responsibly, and helping build a calm science-aware community.

## Useful public participation

Most people do not need to operate telescopes to help. The biggest contributions are education, calm communication, preparedness, and support for science literacy.

## Do not amplify rumors

Unverified claims can cause confusion. Share links to official sources and avoid dramatic language unless official agencies confirm a real emergency.

## Practical workflow

1. **Learn the terms** — understand asteroids, comets, meteoroids, close approaches, and uncertainty.
2. **Follow official sources** — use NASA/JPL, IAWN, ESA, MPC, and local emergency agencies as primary references.
3. **Observe responsibly** — if you are an observer, keep timestamps, locations, equipment notes, and repeatable methods.
4. **Report through proper channels** — use established astronomy and emergency channels instead of social-media guesswork.
5. **Prepare calmly** — build all-hazards family readiness that helps in storms, outages, and rare impact scenarios.
6. **Teach others** — help students, family, and neighbors understand the science without panic.

## Good civilian contributions

- Point people to verified sources.
- Support education and observatory projects.
- Practice family communication and emergency readiness.

## What to avoid

- Posting unsourced impact claims.
- Treating close approaches as confirmed threats.
- Sharing private observer locations without permission.
